## Overview

Innocent archetype is one way to describe a brand that sees life as full of hope and simplicity. As Kamlot and Calmon explain, it can be related to happiness and optimism within the brand-archetype approach. This allows having a foundation for further analysis of the brand without proving its official adoption of the archetype.

## Key characteristics

Hopeful: focuses on a positive outcome.
Simple: uses clear language and avoids needless complexity.
Reassuring: makes it easy to understand what is being offered.

These are ways to interpret the archetype, not a checklist brands must follow. “Simple” should not mean leaving out vital information.

## Brand or website example

Innocent Drinks (https://www.innocentdrinks.co.uk/). In its "things we make” section, the company mentions drinks made from fruits and vegetables, and states that their mission is to help people “do themselves some good.” In my opinion, this clear, positive language is characteristic of the Innocent archetype. This is my analysis of website information, not a statement that this company uses the archetype officially.
Research into the Innocent website also considers how the words and images used influence the brand identity of the company. It is mentioned that brand identity may be complex, as a reminder not to base the interpretation only on the company name, as stated in Kim’s, “Not so ‘innocent’ after all?”.


## Application to web design

One such design choice for an innocent site would be to have the product name and its ingredients listed near the top, along with concise descriptions, and making the next step in the process, which would be looking at more information on the product, clearly visible. The tone used could reinforce the idea, and the listing of the ingredients and nutritional facts ensures that the site is still informative.
