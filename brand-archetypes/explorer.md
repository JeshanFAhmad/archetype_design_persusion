## Overview

The Explorer archetype is driven by a desire for **freedom and discovery**. Explorers want to find out who they are by going out into the world. They look for new places, experiences and ideas, and they resist anything that boxes them in. Explorer brands promise their audience the chance to break away from the everyday and go somewhere new.

## Key characteristics

- **Independent and rugged:** Explorer brands are self-sufficient and at home off the beaten path. They present themselves as tough and capable, and ready for the outdoors or anywhere unfamiliar.
- **Curious and open-minded:** They are always looking for the next experience, place or idea, and they invite their audience to do the same.

You can often spot an Explorer brand by its focus on open landscapes, journeys and self-reliance. It shows where you could go with the product more than the product itself.

## Brand or website example

**[Patagonia](https://www.patagonia.com/)**

Patagonia is a clear example of the Explorer archetype. Its website leads with **wilderness photography**: large images of climbers, surfers and remote landscapes that put the adventure first and the gear second. The imagery connects the brand to freedom and discovery. It also shows the independent, rugged spirit that the brand wants its customers to identify with.

## Application to web design

- **Imagery:** Use immersive, full-bleed landscape photos and video heroes that show wide open spaces. The visuals should make the user feel as if they are already on the journey.
- **Color:** Use an earthy, natural palette, such as forest greens, sky blues, sand and rust. These tones reflect the outdoors and support the brand's rugged, grounded character.
