## Overview

New Wave typography is an experimental approach to graphic design associated with Wolfgang Weingart and the Basel School of Design. It pushed against the strict grids and orderly layouts of Swiss design by treating type, spacing, printing textures, and overlapping images as expressive parts of the composition.

## Key characteristics

- Type that is enlarged, widely spaced, or arranged at unusual angles
- Overlapping text and images
- Grids that are bent, interrupted, or made visible
- Printing textures such as halftone dots and moiré patterns
- Layouts that feel layered and energetic

These are common techniques to look for, not requirements every New Wave design must follow.

## Brand or website example

Wolfgang Weingart, The 20th Century Poster (1984). Made for the Walker Art Center, this poster combines a halftone city image, radiating shapes, and bold lettering. LACMA notes that Weingart layered patterns and text to create depth. In my interpretation, the printing texture becomes part of the subject, making the poster feel both structured and in motion. It’s a poster rather than a website, but it gives us a specific New Wave-related design to study.

## Application to web design

For a modern webpage inspired by New Wave, I could use an oversized heading, a visible grid, and layered image textures. I’d still keep navigation, body text, and buttons readable, and make sure the layout works on a phone. This is my suggested adaptation, not a claim about the original poster.
