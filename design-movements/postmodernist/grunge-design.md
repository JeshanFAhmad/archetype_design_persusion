## Overview

Grunge Design is a graphic style associated with experimental design in the 1990s. It often uses rough textures, layered or distressed type, uneven layouts, and images that look photocopied or collaged. The goal is often to give a design an expressive, handmade, or rebellious mood rather than a polished one. Ray Gun magazine, art directed by David Carson, became a well-known example of experimental typography from this period.

## Key characteristics

- Rough or distressed textures
- Layered, cropped, rotated, or overlapping type
- Uneven layouts that break from a regular grid
- Collage, photocopy-like images, or visible marks
- A deliberately imperfect, handmade feeling

Not every Grunge design uses all of these features.

## Brand or website example

Ray Gun, issue 1 cover (1992), art directed by David Carson. The cover combines a portrait with layered lettering and marks. In my interpretation, the elements feel crowded and imperfect, which gives the cover an energetic, unconventional mood. The magazine is a print example rather than a current website, but it shows how Grunge Design can use type and image together to create a strong visual personality. 

## Application to web design

For a modern music-event page inspired by Grunge Design, I could use a textured background, one rough display headline, and a few overlapping image elements. I would keep event details, navigation, and buttons easy to read and use, especially on a phone. That is my proposed adaptation—the original magazine design was made for print.
